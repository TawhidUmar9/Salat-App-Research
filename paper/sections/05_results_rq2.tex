\section{RQ2: Accuracy Versus Interface}
\label{sec:rq2}

Review-mining literature often treats accuracy and interface polish as one
trade-off users weigh against each other. RQ2 asks whether users punish
inaccuracy more harshly than a poor interface. As a class, they do not ---
but that is the wrong comparison: the average hides a steep severity gradient
inside ``accuracy,'' and star ratings conceal the worst of it.

\subsection{As Classes, They Cost About the Same}

Using M1 (\(n=324{,}687\); all coefficients below survive Benjamini--Hochberg
correction at \(q<0.05\)), the accuracy family --- prayer-time accuracy,
qibla, madhab (the school of Islamic law whose rules set the prayer times),
and calculation method --- averages \(-0.647\) stars against \(-0.600\) for
interface complaints, a gap under a twentieth of a star.

\subsection{But Accuracy Is Not One Thing}

Table~\ref{tab:rq2-coefficients} and Figure~\ref{fig:rq2-coefficients} show why.
Prayer-time accuracy alone costs \(-1.112\) stars (95\% CI
\([-1.142, -1.083]\)), 1.85 times the interface penalty and one of the
costliest complaints in the whole corpus. The family average is dragged down
by milder, rarer complaints: \texttt{madhab} (\(-0.324\)) and qibla
(\(-0.550\)). Getting the prayer time wrong is among the most costly failures
we measure; getting the madhab setting wrong is among the least, and its
coefficient even reverses sign under an ordinal robustness check
(\(\beta=+0.216\), \(p=0.649\)), so we place no weight on it either way.

Qibla complaints, on inspection, are mostly about the compass, not the
calculation: the larger of our two qibla topics (1,771 reviews) describes the
compass giving a different bearing as the phone rotates in place --- a sensor
and calibration problem, not a math error.

\subsection{Star Ratings Hide the Worst Accuracy Defects}

This is RQ2's strongest result, and it is methodological. Of 7,942 reviews with
a negative accuracy complaint, 3,306 (41.6\%) gave four or five stars, and
1,830 (23.0\%) gave the maximum of five. Users routinely report a wrong prayer
time or qibla direction and still rate the app highly in the same review. Any
process that reads defect severity off the star distribution --- which is how
most app-store ranking and triage works --- will systematically under-weight
exactly this class of defect.

One more coefficient is worth flagging: intrusive advertising costs
\(-1.439\) stars, more than any accuracy complaint. We return to advertising
in RQ5 and RQ6.
