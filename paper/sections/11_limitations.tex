\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{No users were studied.} Every finding comes from public
app-store reviews, a self-selected sample skewed toward strong positive and
strong negative experiences. Claims here are claims about what reviewers
chose to write down, not direct claims about religious practice.

\item \textbf{The corpus is English/US-locale and purposively sampled.} It
under-represents the regions where most of the world's Muslims live
(\S\ref{sec:method-corpus}). RQ2's finding that madhab complaints are mild and
cheap should not be read as a global claim.

\item \textbf{Aspect-tagging precision is uneven.} Seven of the 25 aspects with
surviving gold rows fall below 50\%, two at 0\%
(\S\ref{sec:method-measurement}). Every count for these seven is reported
elsewhere as a precision-adjusted estimate with its interval, never as a raw
tagger count.

\item \textbf{Eleven model terms cannot support inference.} Eight are
non-converged rather than precisely estimated nulls, kept in the
Benjamini--Hochberg denominator of 29 for transparency; three more return no
error estimate at all and sit outside it (\S\ref{sec:method-models}). Every
null reported here is cited from a fit that converged.

\item \textbf{The topic model covers a minority of the corpus.} 22.7\% of
modelled documents fall in an outlier bucket and 61\% in one undifferentiated
praise cluster; the 39 labelled topics cover about 16\%.

\item \textbf{The RQ4 sub-model collapsed around one application's privacy
episode.} Six of ten topics concern one application's user data being sold to
US military-linked buyers, a 2020 controversy specific to Muslim Pro, leaving
no room to further resolve menstrual-handling discourse (\S\ref{sec:rq4}).

\item \textbf{Illustrative quotations skew toward one application.} Most of
the reviews quoted in this paper come from Athan, the corpus's largest
tracker-bearing application, because quotation followed the volume of
available text rather than a sampling rule --- the same hazard we identify as
a methodological contribution (\S\ref{sec:rq1-coding}).

\item \textbf{The affect lexicon is unvalidated.} The guilt and motivation
keyword lists behind RQ1's lexical comparison have no gold sample
(\S\ref{sec:method-affect}). RQ1's claim about guilt rests on the hand-coded
passes, which point the same way.

\item \textbf{Two qualitative coding passes, never pooled}, coding 80
reviews between them --- supporting evidence, not a primary contribution.

\item \textbf{Three small-\(N\) case studies are descriptive only.} Inclusion
features ship in 3 of 26 applications; companion hardware and qasr travel
concession each ship in exactly 1. None supports a between-app statistical
estimate.

\item \textbf{Promise-source agreement is weak.} Store descriptions and the
hand-built feature matrix agree at a mean Cohen's \(\kappa\) of 0.272, against
73.1\% mean raw agreement (\S\ref{sec:method-promise}). Any claim depending on
what an application promises should be read with this ceiling in mind.

\item \textbf{The demand-precision sample predates the final extraction.} The
200-row gold sample behind the 91.5\% figure was labelled against an earlier
run (\S\ref{sec:method-demand}).

\item \textbf{Topic modelling covers English-identified text only.} Findings
do not characterise discourse in the languages this filter excluded.

\item \textbf{Reviews are cross-sectional, not longitudinal.} Nothing links a
user's reviews across time, so we can speak to aggregate patterns but not to
how an individual's relationship with an application changes over their
tenure.

\end{itemize}
