\section{RQ4: Bio-Spiritual Inclusion}
\label{sec:rq4}

Islamic law exempts menstruating women from the obligation to pray, and a
prayer tracker that does not represent this exemption misrepresents the
practice it claims to track. RQ4 asks how well this ecosystem serves that
need. The answer is not that designers disagree about how to handle it: almost
no one has built it, the few implementations that exist are not trusted by
their own users, and demand for the feature is voiced, explicitly and
repeatedly, across nearly the whole ecosystem. Inclusion here is an unmet
need, not a contested design question.

\subsection{Scarcity Is the Finding}

The \texttt{women\_period} tagger produces 2,194 aspect-tagged sentences, which
resolve to 2,182 distinct reviews across the 24 of 26 applications carrying any
(Figure~\ref{fig:rq4-women-aspect}). Its precision against gold-standard rows
is only 20\% (95\% CI [6, 51]), so we report an adjusted estimate of roughly
439 genuine menstrual-handling mentions (interval [124, 1{,}119]) rather than
the raw tag count, and that adjusted figure is still spread across nearly the
entire ecosystem. The promise-and-delivery analysis in RQ6 works from a
narrower basis of 1,981 reviews, which is also where the sentiment rates below
come from; we name the three units rather than let one stand in for another. Against
it, only three applications --- Athan, Islamic Habit Tracker, and Pillars ---
ship any form of menstrual handling at all (Figure~\ref{fig:rq4-women-aspect}).
The argument in the rest of this section rests on demand signals and on this
three-app scarcity, neither of which depends on the tagger's precision.

\subsection{Demand Is Explicit, and Concentrated Where the Feature Is Absent}

Demand-pattern extraction, independently validated at 91.5\% precision
(\S\ref{sec:method-demand}), is the firm ground for this research question.
The 272 demand signals tagged to \texttt{women\_period} break down into 161
explicit requests, 51 preference citations, 36 churn statements, 21 explicit
statements of absence, and 3 tenure citations. Of the 272, 216 come from the 16
applications that both lack the feature and draw any demand at all, out of the
23 lacking it. These are signal counts, and one review can carry more than one:
the same demand resolves to 206 distinct reviews, which is the unit RQ6b's
unmet-need index uses (\S\ref{sec:rq6}). The register is direct rather than
incidental:

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``a feature for sisters who are on their monthlies would be nice so
they can keep track''
\end{quote}
\hspace*{1em}--- Muslim Pro: Quran Athan Prayer, 4 stars

\subsection{Having the Feature Does Not Improve Sentiment}

If the gap were purely one of coverage, we would expect the three applications
that ship menstrual handling to show better sentiment on the topic than those
that do not. They do not: mentions run 26.3\% negative in the three apps that
have the feature, against 26.0\% in the 21 lacking it that draw any mention ---
indistinguishable. Shipping menstrual handling is not associated with users
being happier about menstrual handling, which points at the quality of the
existing implementations rather than at availability alone:

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``The only complaint I have is: when I turn on menstrual mode it
doesn't turn off the prayers as it promises to do. Please fix it''
\end{quote}
\hspace*{1em}--- Athan: Prayer Times \& Al Quran, 4 stars

\noindent The RQ1 qualitative coding corroborates this directly and
independently: \emph{menstrual mode unreliable} appears three times in the
single-app coding pass, and \emph{menstrual exemption absent} once in the
cross-app pass, from a different application (\S\ref{sec:rq1-coding}). Across
two independently drawn samples, this is rare first-hand evidence that the
failure is one of execution as well as of coverage.

\subsection{The Between-App Comparison Is Not Estimable}

With only three applications on the treated side, no causal or comparative
claim about star rating is possible, and the data illustrate exactly why. The
review-weighted difference in mean star rating is \(+0.085\) (95\% CI
\([+0.074, +0.096]\), computed directly from the two review pools rather than
from M3, whose \texttt{women\_feature\_score\_diff} term returns neither a
standard error nor a \(p\)-value): apps with the feature average 4.476 stars
against 4.391 for apps without (46,741 reviews across the 3 treated apps
against 277,946 across the 23 untreated). Averaging first by app and then
across apps reverses the sign to \(-0.236\) (4.135 against 4.371). Both
figures are arithmetically correct; they answer different questions. Athan
alone contributes 44,954 of the 46,741 treated reviews, so the review-weighted
figure is close to a single application's rating, while the unweighted figure
is pulled down by Islamic Habit Tracker, the corpus's lowest-rated
application. We report the review-weighted \(+0.085\) as the recorded
estimate, label it explicitly as not estimable rather than as a null result,
and note the sign reversal: a difference that changes direction under a
change of aggregation, on only three treated applications, is the clearest
demonstration available that \(N = 3\) cannot answer this question.

\subsection{The Topic Model Cannot Add Resolution}

A sub-model fit on women's-health and privacy discourse specific to this
research question does not separate cleanly: after hand-labelling, five of
its ten topics carry an identical label describing one application's data
being sold to the US military, and a sixth is a near-duplicate of that label,
so six of ten topics sit on a single theme, holding 58\% of the sub-model's
non-outlier documents (4,788 of 8,285). One application's data-handling
controversy dominates this subset to the point that the sub-model cannot
further resolve menstrual-handling discourse specifically. We report the
recurring themes rather than treat the ten-topic partition as ten separate
findings; we return to this application-specific episode in
\S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Summary}

Bio-spiritual inclusion is an unmet need rather than a contested design
question. Demand is measurable and voiced in the strongest available register,
while supply is three applications, none achieving better sentiment on the
topic than applications with no feature at all. With only three treated apps no
causal or comparative claim is possible, and we make none; the contribution is
the size and shape of the gap, corroborated by two independently drawn coding
passes. \texttt{women\_period} is among the four aspects under 25\% ecosystem
coverage and ranks second on RQ6b's unmet-needs index (\S\ref{sec:rq6}).
