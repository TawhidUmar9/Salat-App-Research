\section{RQ4: Bio-Spiritual Inclusion}
\label{sec:rq4}

Islamic law exempts menstruating women from prayer; a tracker that does not
represent this exemption misrepresents the practice it claims to track. RQ4
asks how well this ecosystem serves that need. Almost no one has built it, the
few implementations are not trusted, and demand is voiced explicitly across
nearly the whole ecosystem. This is an unmet need, not a contested design
choice.

\subsection{Almost No One Has Built It}

The \texttt{women\_period} tagger flags 2,194 sentences across 2,182 reviews in
24 of 26 apps (Figure~\ref{fig:rq4-women-aspect}), but its precision is only
20\% (95\% CI [6, 51]), so the adjusted estimate is closer to 439 genuine
mentions. Only three apps --- Athan, Islamic Habit Tracker, and Pillars ---
ship any menstrual handling at all.

\subsection{Demand Is Explicit}

Demand-pattern extraction, validated at 91.5\% precision
(\S\ref{sec:method-demand}), is firmer ground. The 272 demand signals tagged
to \texttt{women\_period} break down into 161 requests, 51 preference
citations, 36 churn statements, 21 statements of absence, and 3 tenure
citations, mostly from apps lacking the feature:

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``a feature for sisters who are on their monthlies would be nice so
they can keep track''
\end{quote}
\hspace*{1em}--- Muslim Pro: Quran Athan Prayer, 4 stars

\subsection{Having It Does Not Help}

If this were purely a coverage gap, apps that ship menstrual handling should
show better sentiment on the topic. They do not: 26.3\% negative in the three
apps that have it, against 26.0\% in apps that lack it --- indistinguishable.
This points to execution problems, not just availability:

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``The only complaint I have is: when I turn on menstrual mode it
doesn't turn off the prayers as it promises to do. Please fix it''
\end{quote}
\hspace*{1em}--- Athan: Prayer Times \& Al Quran, 4 stars

The RQ1 coding passes independently corroborate this: ``menstrual mode
unreliable'' appears three times in the single-app pass, and ``menstrual
exemption absent'' once in the cross-app pass, from a different app
(\S\ref{sec:rq1-coding}).

\subsection{Why This Cannot Be Tested Causally}

With only three apps on the treated side, no causal claim about star rating is
possible, and the numbers show why. Review-weighted, apps with the feature
average 4.476 stars against 4.391 without (\(+0.085\)); averaged first by app
and then across apps, the sign flips to \(-0.236\) (4.135 against 4.371). Both
are arithmetically correct and answer different questions, since one app
(Athan) contributes nearly all the treated reviews. A result that reverses sign
depending on how it is averaged, on three treated apps, is the clearest
demonstration that \(N=3\) cannot answer this question.

A topic sub-model fit on women's-health discourse also cannot help: six of its
ten topics collapse onto a single unrelated controversy, one app's data-sale
episode, leaving no room to further resolve menstrual-handling discourse
specifically (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

Bio-spiritual inclusion is an unmet need rather than a contested design
question. \texttt{women\_period} is among the four aspects under 25\%
ecosystem coverage and ranks second on RQ6's unmet-need index
(\S\ref{sec:rq6}).
