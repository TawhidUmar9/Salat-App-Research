Figure 1: Three-panel chart of corpus composition. Left: horizontal bar chart of review counts for the top 15 of 26 apps, led by Muslim Pro with roughly 300,000 reviews, dropping to under 10,000 for the smallest of the top 15. Middle: log-scale bar chart of review language mix, dominated by English, then Arabic, Bengali, Indonesian, unknown, Urdu, French, and German. Right: histogram of review length in words (clipped at 60), heavily right-skewed with most reviews under 10 words; a dashed line marks the 5-word threshold used to define the texted-review subset.

Figure 2: Three side-by-side confusion-matrix heatmaps (row-normalized, 0-1) comparing sentiment labels pairwise across 324,687 texted reviews: VADER vs. RoBERTa (Cohen's kappa = 0.385), VADER vs. star-derived label (kappa = 0.210), and RoBERTa vs. star-derived label (kappa = 0.466). Each 3x3 grid crosses Negative/Neutral/Positive labels from one method (rows) against the other (columns). RoBERTa's diagonal is darker (higher agreement) than VADER's, especially on the Positive row (0.98 vs. 0.92 matching the star-derived positive label).

Figure 3: Horizontal bar chart of Cohen's kappa (store-description promise vs. hand-coded feature presence) for 20 app features, sorted descending, colour-coded by agreement band, with raw percent-agreement labelled on each bar. "Women tracking" has the highest kappa (0.62, 92% agreement); four low-volume features (Connect to Google Calendar, Auto Qasr mode, Prayer Times by Location, Timely Reminders) show zero-width bars because kappa is undefined despite 85-96% raw agreement -- a labelled artefact of features present in nearly every app description.

Table 1: Aspect-tagging precision against a 300-row gold-standard sample (11 rows/aspect), listing 25 aspects with corpus n, gold n, point-estimate precision, 95% CI, and a precision-adjusted count for low-precision aspects. Unweighted mean precision is 69.3%; volume-weighted precision is 84.4% (95% CI [79.7, 88.9]). Seven aspects fall below 50% precision (two at 0%, bolded); seventeen aspects at 64% or higher supply most of the analysis.

Figure 4: Three violin plots of per-review sentiment valence (P(positive) - P(negative), range -1 to 1), split by tracker tier: no tracker, tracker only, tracker + score. All three distributions are visibly bimodal, with mass concentrated near +0.7-0.9 and a second, smaller mode near -0.9; horizontal lines mark each tier's mean (~0.20-0.23), which falls in the low-density valley between the two modes rather than describing either group.

Figure 5: Horizontal diverging bar chart, one row per app (23 apps, sorted by guilt share), showing the share of spiritual-affect sentences framed as guilt/anxiety (orange, extending left) versus motivation/discipline (green, extending right). Guilt framing ranges from near 0 to about -0.4 (Muslim Pro highest); motivation framing ranges from about 0.1 to 1.0 (Niya highest, entirely motivational with no guilt framing detected).

Table 2: Two-column comparison of theme prevalence from two independently hand-coded passes of tracker-dissatisfaction reviews, never pooled: Pass 1 (50 reviews, all from the Athan app; n=49 after exclusions) and Pass 2 (30 reviews across the other 22 apps; n=28). Eight themes are listed with count and percentage per pass; two appear only in pass 2. Largest divergence: "prompting failures" rises from 14% (pass 1) to 46% (pass 2), while "redesign as regression" (20%) and "retroactive logging denied" (10%) both drop to 0% in pass 2.

Table 3: Two-column table of M1 regression coefficients (beta, 95% CI) on star rating for 8 complaint aspects surviving BH-FDR correction (n=324,687). Intrusive ads has the largest negative effect (-1.439), followed by prayer-time accuracy (-1.112), stability/bugs (-0.938), reminders/adhan (-0.845), UI design and calculation method (both -0.600), qibla (-0.550), and madhab (-0.324, smallest).

Figure 6: Forest plot of the same eight regression coefficients from Table 3 (effect on star rating, 95% CI), colour-coded into an "Accuracy family" (orange: madhab, qibla, calc_method, prayer_times_accuracy) and a comparison group (blue: UI design, reminders/adhan, stability/bugs, intrusive ads). All effects are negative; intrusive ads is the costliest complaint and madhab the least costly.

Figure 7: Dot chart across 24 apps (sorted by mention volume, log-scaled x-axis from Muslim Pro at ~1,200 mentions down to single digits) of reviews mentioning menstruation or prayer exemptions, with a secondary orange-dot scale (top axis, 0-1) showing each app's negative-sentiment rate on those mentions. Negative-sentiment rates stay low (0.1-0.3) for the highest-volume apps but spike above 0.6 for a few low-volume apps (e.g., Islamic Habit Tracker, Niya).

Figure 8: Scatter plot with fitted regression line and 95% confidence band: complexity-complaint rate (y) against feature count 0-20 (x) across 26 apps, point size scaled to log review volume, each point labelled by app name. The fitted line slopes downward -- apps with fewer features (6-9) draw complexity complaints at roughly 3x the rate (up to 0.15) of apps with the most features (13-14, around 0.02-0.03).

Table 4: Two-column table of the 7 features with at least one broken promise (>40% negative sentiment, n>=30 judgeable mentions, more than one promising app), showing broken/promising app counts and percentage. Monetisation is broken in 5 of 16 promising apps (31%); reminders/adhan in 5 of 20 (25%); mosque finder and forbidden times each in 1 of 4 (25%); widgets 2 of 13 (15%); prayer-time accuracy 2 of 19 (11%); qibla 1 of 18 (6%).

Figure 9: Heatmap of negative-sentiment rate (green = low, red = high, white = not judgeable) for 26 apps (rows) by 19 promised features (columns, e.g. adhkar, monetization, qibla, reminders_adhan, prayer_times_accuracy). An "x" marks cells exceeding the 40%-negative broken-promise threshold. Broken-promise marks concentrate in the monetization and reminders_adhan columns; qibla and reminders_adhan are the most densely populated columns, while companion_hardware and tracker_score have almost no coloured cells.

Table 5: Two-column table ranking the top 5 aspects by unmet-need score (demand-signal volume x number of apps lacking the feature). Companion hardware ranks highest (score 6,725; missing from 25 of 26 apps), followed by menstrual handling/women_period (4,738; missing from 23 apps), mosque finder (3,304), forbidden times (2,805), and nafl prayer times (2,445).

Figure 10: Lollipop chart of the same unmet-need score across all 15 rated aspects, each point labelled with request count and number of apps lacking the feature. Companion hardware and menstrual handling lead by a wide margin (6,725 and 4,738); calc_method and monetization rank lowest (18 and 22), reflecting that almost every app already has those features.
